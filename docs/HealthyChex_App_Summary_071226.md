# HealthyChex — Application Summary

*Preventive-health checklist app · current build: app v4.1 / logic v4.0 (file `HealthyChex_v4_1_Jul2026.html`); specification workbook `HealthyChex_App_v4_0_Jun2026.xlsx` v4.0 · July 2026*

## What HealthyChex is

HealthyChex is a single-file, iPhone-styled web application that turns a patient's demographics, history, and risk factors into a personalized annual preventive-health checklist. It is delivered as one self-contained HTML file (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and inlined icons, with no backend or external assets), runs entirely in the browser, and stores all data locally on the device — nothing is transmitted to a server. It is intended for personal and educational use and is explicitly not positioned as a HIPAA-covered clinical system. Every recommendation is grounded in current guidelines from the USPSTF, CDC/ACIP, the American Cancer Society, and allied bodies, with the supporting citation attached to each item.

## How it works

A patient completes a short intake covering age, biological sex, tobacco use, alcohol use, recreational and injection drug use, sexual history (activity, partner sex, protection, number of partners, prior STI), cardiovascular/chronic-condition risk, family history of early cancer, childhood vaccination status, and chickenpox history. Patients can also import an existing health record through SMART on FHIR (Epic, Cerner, Athenahealth, VA, MEDITECH) using OAuth 2.0 with PKCE, which pre-fills the relevant fields for review.

From those inputs the recommendation engine builds four color-coded categories — Health Checks, Lifestyle Checks, Cancer Checks, and Vaccine Checks. Each card carries a plain-language recommendation, a frequency indicator (Yearly, Routine, One-time, or Discuss with provider), and links to the underlying evidence. Patients mark items complete with a date, and the engine computes the next-due date and displays an on-track, due-soon, or overdue badge. Vaccine items additionally offer a "Decline" option for patients who choose to defer a vaccine; declined items are recorded without a due-date badge, drop out of the completion denominator, and are surfaced to the clinician in the report as declined. A printable physician report summarizes everything, including a recommended-schedule table, and can be saved as a PDF.

## Clinical scope

The Health and Lifestyle categories cover blood pressure, BMI, cholesterol, diabetes, bone density, abdominal aortic aneurysm, depression and anxiety, vision, hearing, skin, and counseling for tobacco, alcohol, drug use, motor-vehicle safety, and sexual health. The Cancer category covers cervical, breast, colorectal, prostate, and lung screening plus BRCA and ovarian genetic-risk assessment, with family-history logic that adjusts start age and frequency. The Vaccine category covers influenza, RSV, COVID-19, Tdap, shingles, pneumococcal, HPV, varicella, hepatitis A and B, MMR, polio, and meningococcal vaccines, gated by age, risk, and childhood-vaccination status.

## v4.1 — RULES extraction + installable PWA (July 2026)

Two behaviour-neutral changes, both proven by the harness (5,700/5,700 identical before and after).

**1. `RULES` extraction.** All 43 clinical threshold literals were lifted out of `gen()` into a single versioned `RULES` object (`_version: '2026-07-17'`) at the top of the script. Covers every screening age band plus the LDCT criteria (age 50–80, ≥20 pack-years, quit ≤15 yrs). Updating a guideline is now a one-line edit in a known place rather than hunting literals through the engine.
- **`RULES` is data, read by fixed logic — never evaluated.** This is deliberate: it keeps any future iOS wrapper inside Apple's interpreted-code carve-out (§3.3.1(B)), where downloaded *data* is fine but downloaded *logic the app evaluates* is a guideline 2.5.2 problem.
- **`oracle.js` deliberately does not read `RULES`** — it keeps thresholds hardcoded independently from the guideline text, so a typo in `RULES` is caught by the harness instead of silently agreed with.
- **New drift check.** `appcore.js` mirrors `RULES`; `run.js` now locates the app HTML, extracts its `RULES`, and compares. Verified by injecting drift (crc start 45→50) — the harness names the diverging rule. This converts the long-documented "appcore can silently drift" fragility into a detected failure, at least for thresholds.

**2. Installable PWA.** Added an inline manifest and 180px apple-touch-icon as **data-URIs**, preserving the single-file architecture (no external `manifest.json`). Plus `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`, `apple-mobile-web-app-title`, `theme-color`, and `viewport-fit=cover`.
- **Why this is a fix, not a feature:** WebKit's ITP deletes all script-writable storage — `localStorage` included — after ~7 days without interaction, and the app stores every profile there. Home-screen apps are exempt and keep their own use counter. Before this, a user who bookmarked rather than installed could lose their profile.
- A dismissible install hint shows **only** when not running standalone (`navigator.standalone` / `display-mode: standalone`), explaining the Share → Add to Home Screen step. Dismissal persists.

## v4.1 — three signed-off cancer-risk engine changes (July 2026)

Wired from `HealthyChex_ThreeChanges_SignOff_071726.xlsx` (all 8 rows approved, no corrections). **This is a recommendation-engine change**, validated in lockstep.

**1. Colorectal family history (new).** Two mutually-exclusive criteria rows added to the family cancer history list, matching the two ACG 2021 tiers:
- *One first-degree relative diagnosed at 60 or older* → `crcfh1` "start at 40 (family history)", usual modality/interval.
- *First-degree relative before 60, or two or more relatives* → `crcfh2` colonoscopy from 40 (or 10 yrs before the youngest diagnosis), **every 5 years**.
Both fire ages 40–75 and **suppress** the standard `crc` card (one colorectal pathway at a time, per sign-off row 1a). Above 75, `crcsel` is unchanged. Per row 1b, the "10 years before youngest dx" rule is stated in the card text rather than computed — the app has no relative-age input by design.

**2. BRCA ungated for men.** The `brca` branch no longer requires `g==='F'`. **Critically, the badge is now sex-aware**: the USPSTF explicitly did not review evidence in men and scopes its Grade B to women, so men see `NCCN · Guideline` and women `USPSTF · Grade B` for the same card — routed through the existing `gradeFor()` resolver. Showing a man a USPSTF grade would have misattributed a recommendation the Task Force never made. The `ovarian` counseling card remains female-only.

**3. Known genetic syndrome (new question).** A Yes/No question placed **outside** the family-history gate (per row 3d — a carrier may answer "no" to family history), revealing BRCA1/2 · Lynch · FAP:
- `brcaknown` — genetics-managed surveillance, referral language.
- `lynch` — colonoscopy every 1–2 years from age 20–25; plus `lynchendo` endometrial surveillance for women.
- `fap` — specialist-managed surveillance, referral.
Lynch and FAP **suppress** both `crc`/`crcsel` and the colorectal family-history cards (row 3b/3c): telling a Lynch carrier to start at 45 is ~20–25 years late.

**Validation.** Harness expanded in lockstep — `appcore.js` mirrors the app, `oracle.js` re-derives all three rules independently from the guideline text, `run.js` generates colorectal tiers and syndrome carriers. **5,700/5,700 decisions concordant (100%), 57/57 types** (up from 5,100 / 51 — six new types: crcfh1, crcfh2, lynch, lynchendo, fap, brcaknown). Plus a **24-case boundary suite**, all passing with app and oracle agreeing independently.

**Latent bug fixed:** the profile-restore path derived chip element IDs as `'c'+type[0]`, which collides for `crc1`/`crc2` (both → `cc`). Replaced with an explicit `FHID` map.

## v4.1 clinical update — LDCT lung-screening eligibility (July 2026)

This round adds structured smoking-history capture that drives true USPSTF 2021 low-dose CT (LDCT) eligibility. **This is a recommendation-engine change** (not UI-only) and was validated separately via the synthetic harness.

- **New intake fields**: When tobacco use is "Former" or "Current," the form reveals *Years smoked* and *Packs per day* (and, for former smokers, *Years since quitting*). A live readout computes pack-years = years × packs/day and shows whether the ≥20 threshold is met.
- **Corrected eligibility logic**: Previously the app offered LDCT to *any* former/current smoker aged 50–80 with no pack-year or quit-timing check (it assumed ≥20 pack-years in the description). The engine now applies the actual USPSTF 2021 Grade B criteria: **age 50–80 AND ≥20 pack-years AND (currently smoking OR quit ≤15 years ago).** This fixes prior over-recommendation.
- **Graceful states via the existing `lungc` code**: smokers 50–80 who haven't entered detail get a "check LDCT eligibility" prompt; those below 20 pack-years get a "below threshold" note; former smokers who quit >15 years ago get a "not indicated (quit >15 yrs)" note. Eligible patients get the `lung` (or `lungfh` with family history) recommendation with their pack-year count shown.
- **Validation**: harness updated in lockstep — `appcore.js` mirrors the new logic (eligibility conditions byte-identical to the app, verified), `oracle.js` re-derives eligibility independently from raw fields (pack-years, quit-timing) per USPSTF, and `run.js` now generates smoking detail spanning the 20-pack-year and 15-year-quit boundaries. Result: **5,100/5,100 decisions concordant (100%, all 51 types)**, plus a 14-case LDCT boundary unit-test suite (all passing).
- **Criteria source**: USPSTF Lung Cancer Screening, 2021 (Grade B) — verified July 2026.

## What changed in this round of updates (v4.0)

Guideline content brought current:

- Cervical cancer screening (ages 30–65) reframed to the American Cancer Society 2025 update (Perkins RB, et al. CA Cancer J Clin. 2026;76(1):e70041). Primary hrHPV testing every 5 years is now presented as the **preferred** strategy; cytology every 3 years and hrHPV + cytology co-testing every 5 years are acceptable alternatives. **Self-collected** hrHPV is added as an acceptable option (repeat every 3 years if HPV-negative), along with screening-exit guidance after age 65 with adequate prior screening. Ages 21–29 are retained on USPSTF 2018 (cytology every 3 years). The pending USPSTF draft (Dec 2024), which aligns by making primary hrHPV every 5 years preferred at 30 and adding patient-collected HPV, is footnoted but not cited as final.

Screening logic corrections:

- Diabetes screening is now age-gated to **≥35 (ADA)**, with earlier screening when a cardiovascular/metabolic risk factor is flagged. This resolves the prior "all adults" over-trigger so the implementation matches its stated ADA rationale, and closes the parked age-gating decision.
- RSV vaccination for ages 50–74 is now shown **only when a qualifying increased-risk condition is flagged**; healthy 50–74 adults with no flag no longer receive the card. Adults ≥75 still receive a one-time recommendation. This removes the second of the two prior deliberate divergences.

Vaccine logic corrections:

- MMR and varicella suppression now also applies ACIP **birth-year presumptive immunity** — MMR suppressed for U.S.-born before 1957, varicella suppressed for U.S.-born before 1980 (birth year is derived from the age the app already collects). The healthcare-worker / pregnancy / immunocompromised exceptions are noted in the card text. These rules are layered on top of the existing childhood-vaccination-completeness and prior-disease suppression.

Sourcing and reference integrity:

- The cholesterol reference key was corrected from `uspstf-chol` to `ahaacc-chol` — the underlying citation is the AHA/ACC cholesterol guideline, not a USPSTF statement. The ≥20 start age is documented as an intentional AHA/ACC-derived divergence from the USPSTF statin window (40–75).
- The specification workbook's **Evidence References sheet was completed** so that every recommendation reference key resolves to a full citation (previously only 14 of ~40 keys were documented). The same key-resolution check passes in the app's in-file reference map.
- Two DOIs were corrected in the app's reference map: abdominal aortic aneurysm to `10.1001/jama.2019.18928` and hepatitis C to `10.1001/jama.2020.1123` (both verified against JAMA/PubMed; the workbook already carried the correct values).

Experience and code fixes (post-specification):

- Hardened the profile loader against a null profile object. The data helper `ld()` can legitimately return null (after a JSON restore, a profile delete, or migration of older single-profile data); `showWS()` and `loadProfileIntoState()` now fall back to the intake form instead of throwing `Cannot read properties of null (reading 'profile')`.

## Validation

The v4.0 engine was validated by running the app's actual recommendation logic (extracted verbatim from the HTML) headlessly against an independently written oracle encoding the USPSTF/ACIP/ACS/AHA-ACC/ADA eligibility rules. Patients were generated with a fixed seed for reproducibility, with every guideline boundary age deliberately injected (e.g., 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, 65/66, 69/70, 74/75/76, 79/80, 85, 26/27, 29/30, 21) alongside randomized demographics and risk factors. Each run compares every patient-by-recommendation decision across 51 recommendation types.

On the 100-patient cohort, concordance was **100.00% (5,100 of 5,100 presence decisions)**, with all 51 recommendation types perfectly concordant and the HIV/HCV recurrence-flag logic (one-time vs. annual) also 100%. The result was stable across five additional random seeds and a 1,000-patient cohort (51,000 decisions, all 100%). A single first-pass discrepancy — the app screening for syphilis in injection-drug users — was traced to an omission in the oracle, not the app: injection drug use is an independent syphilis risk factor (USPSTF/CDC), and the app correctly includes it for syphilis (and HIV/HCV) while correctly excluding it from chlamydia/gonorrhea screening. All v4.0 changes verified cleanly at their boundaries: the diabetes ≥35 gate, RSV 50–74 risk-gating, cervical 30–65 coverage, MMR (before 1957) and varicella (before 1980) birth-year suppression, and the risk-aware HIV/HCV scheduling.

Two divergences from other guideline bodies appear by design rather than as defects: cholesterol screening fires from age 20 (AHA/ACC) rather than only within the USPSTF statin window of 40–75, and hearing screening is offered from age 50 although USPSTF grades it I (insufficient evidence). Neither is a logic defect.

This exercise validates the engine's logic and boundary handling — that the code faithfully implements its intended rules with no mis-gated or off-by-one branches — rather than re-adjudicating the underlying guideline choices, since the oracle and the app necessarily draw on the same guideline sources. The validation harness (extracted engine, independent oracle, and runner) is retained and reproducible (`SEED=… N=… node run.js`).

## Known limitations

The app works only from the inputs it collects, so several guideline criteria are surfaced as guidance for the clinician or patient rather than auto-detected: smoking pack-years and quit-timing (lung CT), body-mass index (diabetes; the app uses the CV/metabolic risk flag as a proxy for "earlier than 35"), travel history, healthcare-worker status, documented HCV or HIV infection, and household hepatitis-B contact. Birth-year presumptive immunity is now applied for MMR (before 1957) and varicella (before 1980), derived from age; however, the healthcare-worker, pregnancy, and immunocompromised exceptions to those birth-year presumptions are surfaced as card-text notes rather than auto-detected, since the app does not collect those statuses. HealthyChex is decision support, not diagnosis, and is not a substitute for professional medical care.
